# Stage HUD

A broadcast HUD for CS2 laid out like a tournament stage broadcast, built for the MatchFrame HUD Library on MatchFrame's public HUD state. Plain rectangles; the neutral panels (score bar, event line, series strip, scoreboard header, killfeed) in a flat royal blue of MatchFrame's own (or dark navy: **Panel colour**); the player cards in the CS side colours (CT blue, T gold) or each team's own; one tall condensed face, Antonio.

What it shows:

- **Top left:** the series strip, the series' maps in order behind arrows: the picking team's logo (else its tag on its colour, or a plain shield when the tag has no letters or digits) or DECIDER, the map's name and the score of maps already played; the live map is lit (with the map's picture from the HUD images behind it), maps to come are dimmed. The minimap sits under it: the map's radar image (or MatchFrame's clean radar) with player numbers, the bomb, bombsites and utility, the same markers as the other MatchFrame HUDs.
- **Top centre:** the event line (the event's name on the left, the match's stage and title on the right, or your own text, and the **trophy** from HUD images in the middle when one is set: standing on the line's bottom edge and rising above it, the texts keeping clear of it), then the score bar: each team's logo (or its tag, or a plain shield for a team with neither) in a 60 px box, the series boxes (one per map needed, lit for each map won), the score centred over a bar in the side's colour under it, and in the middle the round and its clock. While the bomb is down the middle shows the bomb and the seconds left on its fuse (the defuse kit in the CT colour while it is being defused) and a fuse bar drains under the bar. Under the bar: **players alive** (`4 VS 2` on the teams' colours) once someone has gone down in the round, the round's winner and how it was won at round end, MATCH PAUSED during a pause.
- **Top right:** the sponsor scheduled for the **top bar** (its image, else its name; nothing when none is scheduled), and the killfeed under it, at the same place whether or not a sponsor shows (at the top on the compact page): rows tinted with the killer's side colour with killer, assister, weapon, headshot and blind marks and victim, each kill for about seven seconds.
- **Bottom corners:** each team's name on a tab in its colour over its five player cards, the right team mirrored. From the screen edge a card has the observer slot number on a strip in the team's colour, the name with kills and deaths and money, the photo, the main weapon (the primary, else the pistol, else the Zeus or knife: it stays put when the player switches to a knife, grenade, the bomb or the pistol) with the grenades under it, and the health as a big number with the armour and the defuse kit or bomb under it. The card is filled with the team's colour (CT blue and T gold by default, or the team's own) as far as the player's health, grey beyond (a light trail follows a hit). Round kills show as diamonds sitting on the card's top edge above the weapon. The observed player's card is framed in white, drawn over the card without moving anything. A dead player's card shrinks to the name and photo, grey, with a skull (or the HUD images' dead icon) beside the photo; kills, deaths and money stay.
- **Round MVP:** at the round's end and the first seconds of the next round, the round's key player's card grows into a tall card in its place (the cards below keep their places): ROUND MVP, the name, that round's kills and damage, health and armour at the round's end, and the player's picture standing on the card (a cut-out PNG with a transparent background looks best). The key player is CS2's own round MVP when the game data shows it, else the winning side's player with the most kills, then damage.
- **Bottom centre:** the observed player: their **player cam** while it is on air (MatchFrame's player cams, already delayed to match the game), else their picture standing on a transparent backdrop, with their grenades on the left and the ammunition on the right (of the weapon in hand when it has any, else of their main gun) on one line along the bar, over a name bar in the team's colour with the slot number, kills, assists, deaths and health.
- **Names** (cards, observed player, MVP card, scoreboard, killfeed, team tabs) take the room they have and shrink to fit, down to 70 % of their size (62 % on the observed bar); only longer ones end in an ellipsis. Names of 11 or 12 characters such as SPUDWRECKER fit everywhere.
- **Tactical timeouts and tech or match pauses** (only these: freeze time and half time keep the cards and the observed player): the cards and the observed player make way for a full-width **scoreboard** along the bottom. Its blue header has, for each team, the equipment value (the five players' together), the loss bonus (CS2's ladder from the team's losses in a row), the timeouts remaining as pips, the logo, the tag, the series boxes and the score. Under it each player's row of dark cells: photo and name, armour, kit or bomb, grenades, pistol, main weapon and money (green), with the sponsor scheduled for the **scoreboard slot** in the middle (empty when none is). During a tactical timeout a callout over the calling team's end of the bar shows the seconds left, the team and its timeouts left.
- **Labels:** SIMULATION while test data is running, DATA PAUSED when game data stops arriving, REPLAY while a replay is on air.

Players' voice or mute state is never shown: MatchFrame's Team Comms rules keep individual comms off air.

**Icons**, in this order, each tinted with a CSS mask so it takes the panel's colour:

1. **The owner's icon set**, bundled with the package (64 white PNG silhouettes): 34 weapons, the grenades, knife, Zeus, C4, defuse kit, vest and helmet, and the marks for kills, deaths, assists, headshot, health, money, ammunition, the bomb carrier, planting, defusing and defused (its planted mark has a time drawn into it, so the score bar shows the C4 over the live fuse clock instead), a disconnected player, a blinded killer, fire and grenade kills, the timeout (labelled, in the timeout callout) and the match pause.
2. **The icon pack** loaded on the computer, if any (HUD Library → HUD images → Icon pack: Counter-Strike 2's own icons from the operator's copy): whatever the owner's set lacks, such as knife variants and the round-kill star.
3. **MatchFrame's own glyphs and the bundled MIT weapon silhouettes** for anything else.

The package contains no CS2 icon files.

Pictures come from the HUD Library's **HUD images** (default player pictures by side, map logos, the dead icon, the trophy) where a player, map or mark has none of its own. Add only images you have the right to use: an event's own trophy is the organiser's artwork, and the package bundles none. Change the HUD in the HUD Library under **Appearance**: CT and T colours (teams with their own colour keep it), the panel colour (royal blue or dark navy), full names or tags over the cards, the two halves of the event line, and whether the series strip, minimap, killfeed (and how many rows), sponsor slots, players alive, observed player, pause scoreboard (timeouts and pauses), round MVP card, the trophy in the event line and player money show. Variants: **live** (everything), **compact** (score bar, killfeed and observed player) and **replay** (the replay graphic: a REPLAY tag where the score bar sits and a lower third with who made the play, 2K to ACE or the clutch, the weapon and who went down).

Motion runs on `transform`, `opacity` and `clip-path` only, with no blur or backdrop filters, so it stays smooth in an OBS browser source: cards slide in from the screen edges one after another, the scoreboard header opens from the centre and its rows rise in, the players-alive badge drops in under the bar, kills slide in from the right. Only the round clock, the bomb's fuse and the killfeed's timer update several times a second; player cards re-render only when what they show changes.

## Build or change it

The source is in the MatchFrame repository at `apps/hud-designs/src/stage` (React, TypeScript, plain CSS, Vite):

```
pnpm install
pnpm --filter @matchframe/hud-designs dev:stage     # http://localhost:5178/?demo=cycle plays every scene
cd apps/hud-designs
pnpm exec vite build --mode stage && node scripts/finish-packages.mjs stage    # writes dist/stage
```

`?demo=<scene>` shows one sample state (`freeze`, `live`, `kill`, `plant`, `bomb`, `defuse`, `over`, `timeout`, `mvp`, `nocam`, `pause`: a tech pause); `?demo=cycle&every=3000` plays them in turn. Test states (not in the cycle): `edge` (long names: Spudwrecker, 16 and 32 characters, twelve W's, NORTHERN LIGHTS ESPORTS; a knife, a grenade and a pistol in hand next to bought guns; a pistol-only player; a team without a logo; black-silhouette default pictures; the observed player on the right team), `edgebomb` (the bomb planted with its timer while the data has paused; a team whose tag is "?"), `edgeover` (the round won, Spudwrecker observed and its MVP, the 32-character name in the killfeed) and `edgeboard` (a tactical timeout's scoreboard with those names); `--scenes edge,edgebomb,edgeover,edgeboard` takes them with `scripts/shots.mjs`. Add `&art=1` for sample HUD images, `&radar=1` for a sample radar (`&radar=mirage`: Mirage's real radar image, served by the screenshot scripts), `&logo=1` for sample team emblems, `&sponsor=1` for a made-up sponsor (`&sponsor=none` for none, as live with nothing scheduled), `&trophy=1` for a sample trophy (a generic cup drawn for MatchFrame), `&score=13-1` for another map score, `&bo=5` (or `1`, `3`, `7`) for another series length and `&cam=0` (or the `nocam` scene) to leave out the stand-in player cam. `node scripts/shots.mjs stage <dir> --query "art=1&radar=1&logo=1"` screenshots every scene. To make your own HUD from it, set your own `id`, `name` and `version` in `package-files/stage/manifest.json`, build, then use **Import HUD Folder** on `dist/stage` (or zip it and use **Import HUD ZIP**).

Rules every HUD package follows: transparent `html` and `body` backgrounds; the page reads `output`, `variant` and `t` from its query string and opens the read-only `/overlay-ws` socket; settings arrive in `state.settings` with every snapshot; a visible label while `state.simulated` is true; only static files (plus `/hud-runtime/cams.js`, which the HUD server provides for player cams).
