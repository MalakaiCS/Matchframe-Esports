# Matchframe Esports 0.9.5-dev — development build

**Unsigned development build. Checked automatically on Linux and on a Windows CI runner. Not yet seen in CS2 on your PC.**

**Coming from 0.9.4:** the program updates itself. Press **Restart now** in the status bar when the update has downloaded.

## Smooth moving cinematics

Cameras moving round the map used to jolt twice a second. Their movement followed a smooth curve, but their turning went in straight lines from one point of the path to the next, so the turning speed jumped every half second. Now movement and turning follow the same smooth curve.

Measured over all 1,487 built-in camera paths:

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Paths whose turning jolts by more than 20 °/s within one frame | 169 | 0 |
| Biggest jolt on any path | 61 °/s | 5 °/s |

## Freeze time shows only where players are

A map camera now counts a player only when they're in its picture for the whole shot (even while it turns slowly) and in clear sight. Before, being in the area the camera was meant for was enough, even if the camera looked the other way. Team shots are framed from where the players stand, as before.

## Nade cams on players, and they stay

- **Players:** a nade cam is only used when someone is near the grenade and in that camera's picture.
- **Hold:** once it cuts to a nade cam, it stays at least 2.5 s, until about 2 s after the grenade goes off. Before, it left the moment a flash or HE went off. Only the bomb (a defuse, or the last seconds) cuts away sooner.

## The free camera no longer stays on air

Sometimes a cinematic ended in CS2's free camera, near the last player, and stayed there.

This happened when the player CS2 was on died during a cinematic (or CS2 moved to another player by itself). Matchframe took the new player as already on screen. On some CS2 builds the game data names a player even while the free camera is on, so nothing told CS2 to leave it.

Now Matchframe selects a player as soon as that happens, which always leaves the free camera.

## kaiHUD with high graphics: Cap CS2's frame rate

On high settings with no frame cap, CS2 takes all of the graphics card, and a HUD that needs a lot of drawing, like kaiHUD, has to wait its turn. That's why it lagged on high but not on low.

**HUD Library → Game options → Cap CS2's frame rate** now sends `fps_max`. For a 60 fps stream, pick **120**: the game stays smooth, the card has room for the HUD and OBS, and you can keep high graphics.

If you use OBS, running it as administrator also gives it priority on the graphics card.
