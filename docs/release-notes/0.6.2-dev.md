# Matchframe Esports 0.6.2-dev — development build

**Unsigned development build. Checked automatically on Linux and on a Windows CI runner, against CS2's own map geometry and a stand-in CS2 console. Not yet used against a real CS2 client.**

**Coming from 0.6.1:** the program updates itself. Press **Restart now** in the status bar when the update has downloaded. Nothing to change.

## Fixed: cinematics in the ground

On Mirage the cinematics ended up in the ground. The cause was in 0.6.1's new smooth turning, not in the cameras:

- 0.6.1 turned each freeze-time shot with CS2's `+left` and `+right` commands. In CS2 those are the strafe keys (A and D), not turning. So the camera slid sideways, through walls and under floors, for about a second before Matchframe noticed that it wasn't turning.
- 0.6.2 sends CS2 no movement keys at all. Matchframe moves the camera itself, 64 positions a second, slowly enough that each step is far below a pixel.

Every camera on all nine maps was also checked against CS2's own collision geometry (the build the map data comes from):

- **Inside geometry or under a floor:** none.
- **Looking into a wall:** 25 cameras had a wall filling a third or more of the picture, mostly in tunnels, stairs and small rooms. 12 were removed and 13 moved to better spots. 379 built-in cameras remain.

## Freeze time like a broadcast: team shots

Freeze time now shows each team the way a broadcast does, framed from where its players actually stand. The map's spawn cameras looked at areas rather than players; on Mirage, walls filled half the picture and the players were small.

- **Placement:** the camera is a little above head height, a few hundred units away. All five players are in the picture and in sight, seen from the side they face where possible.
- **Checks:** for each spot, Matchframe checks the map's free-space grid, built from CS2's own collision geometry. The camera needs room round it and floor below it, nothing between the lens and the team, and nobody standing in the foreground.
- **Motion:** while on air, the camera glides about 10° round the team and slightly closer, always looking at them. The whole glide is checked the same way.
- **Order:** one team, then the other, sharing the freeze time. The second shot runs until the round goes live, then first person.
- **Fallbacks:** if no spot frames a team, the map's spawn cameras show it, turning slowly. A player who has walked off or is on another floor is left out of their team's shot.
- **Pauses:** a tactical or technical pause holds a still shot of the team that called it until the round goes live.

Checked against CS2's geometry for both spawns of all nine maps (Overpass's CT spawn has no area in the map data to place players in):

- every spawn gets a team shot;
- along every glide, the camera is in open space and sees the players it frames.
