## Team Comms (new, off until you switch it on)

Listen in on a team's TeamSpeak comms on the broadcast, delayed to match the gameplay, and make a separate recording of one team's comms with the gameplay and a speaker strip.

It's under **Live Control → Team Comms**. Nothing changes on your stream until you switch it on there (under **Bridge and TeamSpeak plugin**) and press **Listen In**.

**What you need on the production PC:**
- **TeamSpeak 3** (not TeamSpeak 5 or 6), with one tab in each team's channel. Use **Install TeamSpeak plugin** on the page, with TeamSpeak closed. Then enable *MatchFrame Team Comms* in TeamSpeak under Tools → Options → Add-ons.
- **DistroAV** in OBS (with the NDI 6 Runtime it asks for). MatchFrame sends the comms to OBS through it.
- **Source Record** in OBS (free plugin by exeldro), only for the separate comms recording.

The **Setup checklist** at the top of the page shows each of these as done or not, with the steps.

**On the broadcast:**
- **Listen In** puts one team's comms on air, delayed by your chosen time so they line up with the gameplay. **End Listen-In** closes it. **Mute All Comms** silences the broadcast comms at once.
- You choose who is included, separately for the broadcast and the recording. Anyone who joins is left out until you include them, so a coach or manager is never heard by accident.
- The casters go down while a team is on air, and come back after.
- A **TEAM COMMS** banner with the team's name and logo shows on the stream. It never shows who is speaking.
- **Your headphones:** you can listen to any player privately before putting a team on air. That sound never goes to OBS.
- After a restart of TeamSpeak, OBS or MatchFrame, comms never go back on air by themselves.

**The separate comms recording:**
- **Set up the recording scene** makes its own OBS scene: your gameplay and HUD, the team's comms, and a strip of hexagon portraits that light up green when someone talks.
- **Start Recording** records it in the background while your stream cuts to casters, replays or breaks. The recording stays on gameplay.
- The recording only has sound while OBS is streaming or recording. MatchFrame warns you if it isn't.

**Timing:** **Calibration** helps you set the delay. A player says "mark" and you press **Seen** when it shows in the gameplay. A beep test then fine-tunes the strip against a short recording.

**Presets:** save each event's setup (who's who, who's included, levels, delay) and load it next time.

**Tested here, not yet on Windows:**
- Real TeamSpeak 3: two teams of 7 for 47 minutes, with no lost audio and no voices in the wrong place.
- OBS 30 with Source Record: recordings stayed on gameplay throughout, and every time someone talked their portrait lit.
- The plugin also builds for Windows.

**Still to be checked on your PC:** the plugin in TeamSpeak on Windows, DistroAV, your headphones, the casters going down, Source Record with your graphics card, and everything running together with CS2.

**Please try it before a match day:** with a few people talking in each team channel, try Listen In, End and Mute All. Make a 10-minute recording with the stream on, then send me a support file and the recording.
