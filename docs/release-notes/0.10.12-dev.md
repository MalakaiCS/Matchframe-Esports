## Smooth replays and clean sound from the replay feed

These are fixes for the main PC's recording of the replay PC's picture and sound (NDI, such as NDI Screen Capture at 1080p60), which the replays are cut from.

**Choppy replays:**
- **Before:** each frame was timed by when it reached the recorder, so frames arriving unevenly were repeated or skipped to fit 60 a second.
- **Now:** frames are timed by the replay PC's own clock, which NDI sends with each frame.
- **Measured:** on a 1080p60 test, repeats and skips went from about 300 in 40 seconds to none, and to under 25 with the main PC heavily loaded.

**Dropped frames:**
- **Before:** the recorder threw away any frame that arrived while the previous one was still being taken in. That dropped about one frame in six, even with nothing else running.
- **Now:** frames wait up to a quarter of a second instead. All 60 frames a second are recorded.

**Crackly sound:**
- **Before:** the sound was lined up with timestamps that wobble by 10–25 ms from frame to frame. Each wobble put in a sliver of silence or cut a few samples, and that's the crackle.
- **Now:** the sound is written as it comes. Only a real dropout (a fifth of a second or more) is filled, with a fade. Small drifts against the picture are corrected by playing 0.3% faster or slower, which you can't hear.
- **Measured:** a test tone went from up to 247 breaks in 30 seconds to none.

## Where frames are lost: the replay feed panel

The replay feed panel under **Replays & Production** now shows:
- the encoder in use;
- "52 of 60 frames a second arriving";
- each kind of loss with a hint:
  - **Not arriving from the replay PC:** use wired gigabit, and cap CS2's frame rate on the replay PC.
  - **This computer's encoder is behind:** choose your graphics card's encoder.
  - **Frames repeated to fill gaps.**

The support file carries the same numbers.
